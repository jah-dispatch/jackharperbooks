Hemingway Falls by Jack Harper

Two timelines. One rusted copper mailbox. A connection that defies decades.

Deep within the misty douglas firs outside Seattle, an abandoned lake house holds a secret left behind in a cast-copper mailbox. When neurology intern Brian Bellvue leaves a quiet, sentimental letter in the past, he never expects a response—let alone one from Richard Arthur Dixon, a traveling journalist writing from years in the future.

As hand-written letters bridge the impossible divide between their eras, Brian scrambles to pioneer a clinical protocol for a rare neurological condition—while Richard holds the ultimate historical truth of Brian's work, and the quiet lakeside town of Hemingway Falls draws them both toward an unforgettable reckoning.

Brian Bellvue

Grounded, perceptive, and fiercely dedicated to his patients, neurology intern Dr. Brian Bellvue spends his days navigating the high-stakes corridors of a major Seattle hospital and his quiet moments retreating to the mist-shrouded shores of Lake Hemingway.

When an unexplained perceptual phenomenon and an impossible correspondence through an old copper mailbox upend everything he understands about medicine and time, Brian must balance clinical rigor with an undeniable pull toward a man he has never met in person.

Rick Dixon

Sharp-tongued, privately affluent, and restless beneath the polished exterior of a veteran Seattle journalist, Richard Arthur Dixon thought he had outrun every sentimental impulse—until a weathered mailbox outside Hemingway Falls delivers a letter that shouldn't exist.

Trading breaking-news deadlines for quiet afternoons in a tucked-away Issaquah bookshop and late-night letters across time, Rick discovers that the voice writing back from the past knows him better than anyone in his own century.
