Chasing Ghosts Across Timelines in Hemingway Falls

Two timelines, one rusted copper mailbox, and the quiet mechanics of longing.

Deep within the misty Douglas-firs outside Seattle, an abandoned cabin holds a secret left behind in a cast-copper mailbox. When I first sat down to plan Hemingway Falls, I knew the story couldn't rely on flashy sci-fi machinery. The bridge between Brian Bellvue and Rick Dixon needed to feel tactile. As if ink pressed into heavy cotton paper.

Writing a dual-timeline romance where two men communicate strictly through letters requires careful pacing. Every note left inside that mailbox carries the weight of weeks or years. If Brian asks a question in his timeline, Rick holds the historical truth of how that moment was resolved decades later.

"To write a letter is to trust that the person on the other side of time is still listening."

Alongside the correspondence, Brian is scrambling to pioneer a clinical protocol for a rare neurological condition known as Vespertine Neural Drift. Grounding those hospital chapters in authentic detail is making the quiet solitude of the lake house feel even more urgent on the page.

Thank you to everyone who has expressed interest in Hemingway Falls. Stay tuned for my next blog post, where I’ll host my own character POV interview and answer questions through the vernacular of these two extraordinary gay men, and how their stories are shaping up to be an insufferable love-fest spanning twenty years.
